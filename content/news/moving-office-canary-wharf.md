If you have ever tried to book a loading bay in Canary Wharf at short notice, you already know that moving office here is not like moving office anywhere else in London. The towers on the estate and across the Isle of Dogs run on managed access. Nothing comes in or out of a building without a slot, a service lift and someone from building management expecting you. Turn up with a van and a crew and no booking, and the move does not start.

That is not a reason to dread an E14 office move. It is a reason to plan it in a particular order. Here is what office managers and business owners need to have in place.

## Start with the building, not the van

In Canary Wharf the first call should be to building management at both addresses, because their rules set the timetable for everything else.

Ask three things before you fix a date:

- Which days and hours are available for removals, and whether out-of-hours slots are offered.
- How far ahead the loading bay and service lift need to be booked, and whether one booking covers both.
- What paperwork they want before granting access, such as a method statement and risk assessment, proof of insurance or a list of crew names.

Write the answers down and share them with whoever is quoting for your move. Removals Nationwide produces a written move plan from a free on-site survey, and that plan is only as good as the access information behind it. The [office removals service](/office-removals/) is built around this kind of building liaison, so the more you know about the slots on offer, the more accurate the quote will be.

## Loading bays, service lifts and estate permits

Three pieces of access logistics decide whether a Canary Wharf move runs smoothly.

**The loading bay.** Towers here do not have a kerb you can pull up to. Loading happens in a managed bay shared with couriers and fit-out contractors. Book the slot as early as the building allows, and book the full window you need rather than the minimum.

**The service lift.** Passenger lifts are usually off limits for crates and furniture. The service lift is booked alongside the bay, often with a porter or security officer to operate it. Find out its dimensions and weight limit at the survey stage. A desk pod that fits your current lift may not fit the one at the destination, and finding that out on move day is expensive.

**Parking and permits.** On the Canary Wharf estate and much of the Isle of Dogs, parking is controlled by private estate management rather than the council alone. Instead of a council bay suspension, you are usually looking at an estate permit for the loading vehicle, arranged through the building or estate team. Elsewhere in [Tower Hamlets](/areas/tower-hamlets/) a council suspension may still apply, and boroughs need notice measured in working days, not hours. Whichever applies, the removals company's operations team should arrange it as soon as your date is confirmed and build it into the quote. The A13 and Commercial Road approaches are busy, so the vehicle's arrival needs timing around the booked slot too.

## Out of hours is the norm, not the exception

Most buildings on the estate push removals into evenings and weekends, and most businesses prefer it. An office of fifty people idle for half a day costs far more than an evening crew premium.

Plan for the move to complete while the office is empty. The usual pattern is:

- Friday evening: IT disconnected by your own team, crates sealed, furniture dismantled and loaded.
- Saturday: unload at the new address, furniture rebuilt to the floor plan, IT positioned first so reconnection can start.
- Sunday: your IT lead tests systems, snagging is dealt with, packing waste is removed.
- Monday morning: staff arrive at a working office.

Larger organisations spread this over several weekends in a phased move, department by department. Whichever pattern you choose, get out-of-hours access confirmed in writing at both ends.

## IT first, furniture second

IT is the highest-risk part of any office move and the part that decides whether Monday morning works. Agree the sequence with your IT lead before the crew is booked.

In practice, your IT team disconnects equipment to its own protocol, labels and bundles cabling, and tells the crew the order in which servers, switches and workstations should be packed. Equipment travels in anti-static IT crates and is placed first at the new address, giving your team the longest window to reconnect and test.

Two points that often get missed:

- Confirm the comms room at the new address is powered, cooled and connected before move day.
- Decide what is not coming with you. Decommissioned monitors, printers and servers can be collected on the same job and recycled to WEEE standards by a registered waste carrier.

## Crates, packing and labelling

Cardboard boxes and a managed service lift are a poor match. Boxes crush, stack badly on a trolley and generate waste that has to leave through the same booked slot. Lidded plastic crates stack cleanly and go back to the hire company afterwards. [Crate hire](/crate-hire/) is charged by the week, delivered in advance and collected once you have unpacked. Order enough for every desk plus a margin for kitchens and shared storage.

Labelling is where moves are won or lost. Give every desk position at the new office a code that matches the floor plan, put that code on every crate and piece of furniture belonging to it, and give the crew a copy of the plan. If your team does not have time to pack, a professional [packing service](/packing-service/) can wrap files, equipment and furniture far faster and with fewer breakages.

## Storage for phased moves and gaps

It is common for a Canary Wharf lease to end before the new space is ready, or for a fit-out to run over. Rather than compress the whole move into one night or pay for an empty floor, use storage to bridge the gap.

Containerised business storage in a 24/7 CCTV-monitored facility can hold furniture, archives and spare equipment for weeks or months, and it suits phased moves: items leave the old office in stages and come out of [storage](/storage/) as each floor at the new address is signed off. Ask for it to be priced within the move quote.

## Paperwork building management will ask for

Expect to provide the following before access is granted. Have it ready early and the move will not stall on an email thread.

- A method statement and risk assessment (RAMS) describing how the crew will work and how the building will be protected. Removals Nationwide produces these as part of pre-move planning when the requirement is flagged at the survey.
- Proof of goods-in-transit and public liability insurance. Public liability matters here because it covers damage to the building itself, such as lift interiors, floors and door frames.
- Crew names and vehicle registrations for security.
- Written confirmation of the loading bay and service lift slots at both addresses.

Keep copies. If a slot is disputed on the night, the written confirmation is what gets the lift doors open.

## A simple timeline for an E14 office move

Every move is different, but this order of work suits most Canary Wharf offices:

1. Speak to building management at both addresses and note the access rules, slots and paperwork required.
2. Book a free on-site survey and get a fixed written quote covering IT, furniture, access and any storage.
3. Confirm the date, then book loading bays, service lifts and the estate permit or council suspension straight away.
4. Agree the IT sequence with your IT lead and order crates to arrive a week before the move.
5. Issue the labelling plan to staff and set a packing deadline.
6. Submit RAMS, insurance and crew details to both buildings.
7. Move out of hours, position IT first, rebuild furniture to plan, and clear waste and redundant equipment on the same job.

Done in that order, a Canary Wharf move is no harder than any other, just more dependent on early bookings. If you would rather hand over the planning, [book a survey](/bookservice/) and have the access logistics handled as part of the move.
