An office relocation is usually a sign that a business is doing well. The team has outgrown the current space, a better connected part of London has become affordable, or the firm wants a layout that suits hybrid working rather than rows of desks that sit empty three days a week. The risk is the fortnight in between, where nobody can find the printer, the phones ring out and the sales team is waiting on a broadband engineer.

Downtime is the real cost of an office move, and most of it is avoidable if the relocation is run as a project with an owner, a timeline and a plan for keeping the work going.

## Start Earlier Than Feels Necessary

Most London office moves are planned too late. The lease end is known a year ahead, yet the removal is often booked a few weeks before it. Working back from move day, a realistic timeline is:

- **Three to four months out:** confirm the new premises, agree the floor plan and book a survey so the removals company can see the IT, the furniture to dismantle and the access at both buildings.
- **Two months out:** fix the date, book the crew, order crates, and give your IT provider a firm date for connectivity at the new address.
- **One month out:** start the declutter and archive, brief staff, and tell clients and suppliers about the change of address.
- **Two weeks out:** confirm goods lift bookings, loading bay reservations and any parking suspension, which councils typically need 2 to 5 working days to process.
- **Move week:** pack, label, move, reconnect.

## Appoint One Move Lead

A move with no owner becomes everybody's second job and nobody's first. Appoint one move lead with the authority to make decisions and the time to do it: often the office manager or a director in a small firm, a facilities manager in a larger one.

The move lead holds the master timeline, is the single point of contact for the removals company, both landlords, the IT provider and the building managers, signs off the floor plan and labelling scheme so every crate has a destination, and makes the calls on move day.

Ask the same of the removals company. A named move coordinator who has walked both buildings is worth more than a cheaper quote from a firm that sends whoever is free.

## Communicate With Staff and Clients Early

People tolerate disruption they have been told about. Staff should hear about the move from management before they hear it as a rumour, and should know when to clear their desks, what to pack themselves, where their belongings will be on day one and whether to work from the office or home during move week. A short written brief, a reminder the week before and a floor plan with names on desks covers it.

Clients and suppliers need less detail but earlier notice. Update the address on your website, email signatures, invoices and Google Business Profile before the move. Tell key accounts directly, warn them if response will be slower on the move days, and set up mail redirection for anyone you missed.

## Phase the Move Where It Helps

Not everything has to move in one weekend. For an office of more than a couple of dozen people, moving in stages removes most of the disruption.

- **By department:** move teams that can work remotely first and client-facing teams last, once the new office is proven.
- **By floor or zone:** clear one floor per evening so the rest of the building keeps working.
- **Furniture first, people later:** move spare furniture, archive files and stored equipment ahead so the final weekend is only desks, chairs and live IT.

## Treat IT Cut-over as the Critical Path

Furniture can arrive a day late and the business survives. If the internet is not working on Monday morning, it does not. Connectivity belongs at the centre of the plan.

- **Lead times.** A new leased line or fibre connection can take weeks to install. Order it as soon as the premises are confirmed, with a mobile data backup in case the line is not live.
- **Who disconnects and reconnects.** Your IT team disconnects and labels equipment to their own protocol before the crew packs it. At the new office, IT is unloaded and positioned first so reconnection starts early.
- **Servers and comms hardware.** Switches, servers and UPS units need anti-static crates, bundled cabling and a crew that has moved them before. If you are moving to cloud services anyway, do it first.
- **Phones.** Check whether your numbers can move with you; older lines may need porting, which has its own lead time.

Plan the cut-over for a Friday evening or a weekend wherever possible. The crew works in an empty office, IT has the weekend to test, and staff arrive on Monday at a working desk. Removals Nationwide works 7 days a week, including evenings, and sequences IT first at both ends.

## Declutter and Archive Before Anything Is Packed

Every item you move costs crew time, crate space and unpacking effort. Four to six weeks before the move, go through the office with three categories in mind:

- **Bin or recycle:** broken chairs, obsolete printers, out-of-date brochures, cables for equipment that left years ago. Decommissioned IT should be recycled to WEEE standards by a registered waste carrier.
- **Archive:** paper records you must keep but never open. Box and label them and send them to storage rather than giving them a shelf in the new office.
- **Move:** what is left, which is what the business actually uses.

Shred confidential documents and wipe old drives at this stage, with the paperwork to prove it. For the packing itself, reusable lidded crates are faster to fill and protect contents better than cardboard. ## What Keeps Working During Move Week

Decide in advance what the business must be able to do during move week and plan around it. For most firms that means phones answered, even from mobiles or a diverted number; email and shared files available, a strong reason to have cloud services in place beforehand; one or two people reachable by clients; and payroll, invoicing and anything with a fixed deadline done early.

Move week is not the week for launches, big client meetings or new starters. If staff can work from home either side of the move, let them; an empty office is faster to pack and unpack. Keep a first-day kit with the move lead: router, network cables, keys, a printed floor plan and the numbers for the landlord, the IT provider and the removals coordinator.

## The First Week in the New Office

The move is not over when the van leaves. The first week decides whether people settle or spend a month frustrated.

- **Day one:** have the move lead and IT on site before staff arrive. Check every desk has power and a network connection, and keep a visible list of what is still outstanding.
- **Days two and three:** fix the snags: missing crates, a desk in the wrong place, a meeting room with no screen cable. Have the removals company collect empty crates promptly.
- **End of the week:** a short review with the team on what still needs sorting, and update the building induction, fire plan and health and safety paperwork for the new premises.
