Moving home in winter brings challenges that simply do not apply in summer. Rain, sleet and melting snow all raise the risk of water damage, and a wet London pavement in January is a very different loading area from a dry one in July. Cold adds its own problems: wood shrinks, glue goes brittle and electronics can fail if they are switched on too soon. None of this is a reason to avoid a winter move. It just means furniture needs more thought before, during and after the day.

## Why damp and cold damage furniture

Different materials fail in different ways, so it helps to know what you are protecting against.

- **Solid wood and veneer.** Wood swells in damp air and shrinks as it dries. Repeated cycles loosen joints, lift veneer and crack lacquer. A table carried through rain and then stood by a radiator can split within days.
- **Upholstery.** Fabric that gets wet and is then wrapped tightly will not dry. The result is staining, a musty smell and, at worst, mould in the padding.
- **Leather.** Leather stiffens in the cold and marks when wet. Dried too quickly it loses its oils and cracks along the seams.
- **Electronics.** Televisions and computers sit safely in the cold, but condensation forms inside them in a warm room. Switching them on while that moisture is present can cause permanent damage.
- **Mattresses.** A mattress soaks up water like a sponge and dries very slowly. A wet one is often beyond saving.

## Preparing items before moving day

Clean and dry everything first. Wipe down wooden surfaces, vacuum upholstery and let anything damp dry out fully in a heated room. Moisture trapped under wrapping is far more damaging than rain that lands on the outside.

Empty drawers and cupboards, then tape the drawers shut or remove them. Dismantle beds and large wardrobes where you can: smaller sections are easier to keep covered and quicker to carry through a doorway in a downpour. Keep fixings in a labelled bag taped to the item.

Back up computers, photograph cable connections before unplugging anything and remove batteries from remotes. Let washing machines drain and leave the door open for a day so the drum is dry before it is wrapped.

## Wrapping and protective materials

One principle applies throughout: the outer layer should shed water and the inner layer should let the item breathe.

- **Furniture blankets** are the first line of defence for sofas, tables and wardrobes. They cushion knocks and absorb a surprising amount of rain.
- **Stretch wrap** goes over the blanket, never directly on wood or leather. On its own it traps moisture and can lift a finish. Over a blanket it holds everything in place and keeps rain off.
- **Corrugated card** protects corners, table edges and glass doors on narrow stairs. Tape it to the wrapping, not to the furniture.
- **Mattress covers and sofa covers** are worth the small cost in winter. A sealed cover is the only reliable way to keep a mattress dry between the front door and the van.
- **Bubble wrap** suits mirrors, glass shelves, pictures and electronics. Wrap screens in a soft cloth first so the bubbles do not mark them.
- **Silica gel packs** absorb the moisture that builds up inside sealed boxes of electronics, books and anything going into storage.
- **Double-wall boxes** hold their shape when damp far better than secondhand boxes, where water damage is invisible until the bottom gives way.

## Keeping floors and doorways dry

Wet floors and feet tracking grit through both properties cause as much damage as rain. Lay runners or flattened card along the route from the front door to the van. Put an absorbent mat inside each door and swap it when soaked. Keep old towels by the entrance for drying anything caught in a shower, and prop doors open so items are not held in the rain. If it is icy, clear and grit the path and steps early.

When loading in rain, work in short bursts and keep the van doors closed between trips. Stage wrapped items in the hallway so each one goes straight from a dry space into the van. Load the most vulnerable pieces, such as mattresses, upholstered seating and veneered furniture, at the back of the van, furthest from the doors.

## Van and transit conditions

A closed removal van keeps the rain off, but the inside is as cold as the street. Expect furniture to arrive chilled. That is fine as long as it is dry and allowed to warm up slowly at the other end.

Dry anything that got wet on the way out before it is loaded. A damp blanket wrapped round a sofa sits against the fabric for the whole journey. Strap items so they cannot shift and rub, and keep boxes of electronics off the van floor in case water tracks in from the doors.

## Letting items acclimatise at the new home

Resist the urge to turn the heating up to full and unwrap everything at once.

Unwrap wooden and leather furniture as soon as it is indoors so trapped moisture can escape, then let it reach room temperature gradually, away from radiators, for the first day or two. Wipe off surface water with a dry cloth and buff leather once it has warmed. Give upholstered pieces air rather than pushing them against a cold external wall on day one.

Electronics need the most patience. Leave televisions, computers and speakers unwrapped in the room for several hours, ideally overnight, before plugging them in. This gives any condensation inside time to evaporate.

Take mattress covers off as soon as the bed is in place. If a mattress did get damp, stand it on its side in a warm, ventilated room until completely dry.

## Winter move checklist

Run through this list in the week before the move.

- Clean and fully dry every item, including cupboard interiors and appliance drums
- Empty drawers, tape them shut or remove them, and dismantle beds and wardrobes
- Back up computers, photograph cable layouts and remove batteries
- Order boxes, bubble wrap, tape and mattress covers a few days ahead
- Wrap in order: blanket first, stretch wrap over the top, card on corners
- Put silica gel packs in boxes of electronics and anything going into storage
- Prepare runners, mats and towels at both properties, and grit icy paths
- Load mattresses and upholstery at the back of the van
- Unwrap wood and leather on arrival and keep it away from radiators
- Leave electronics switched off for several hours before plugging them in
- Book storage in advance if the new home may not be ready

A winter move does not have to mean damaged furniture. With dry preparation, the right wrapping and patience at the new address, your belongings should arrive in the condition they left.
