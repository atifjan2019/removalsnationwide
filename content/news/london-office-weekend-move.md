Moving an office in London is never just a case of packing boxes and hiring a van. It is a balancing act. You have staff who need to know where they are sitting on Monday, clients who still expect replies by the end of the day, and a city whose streets, buildings and parking rules were not designed with removal lorries in mind. A weekend move is the usual way to square all of that, because the work happens while the office is empty. It only works if the preparation is done properly beforehand. This guide sets out what to sort, and when.

## Why weekends suit London office moves

The main cost of an office move is rarely the removal invoice. It is the lost working time. A team that cannot log in for half a day costs far more than the difference between two quotes. Moving on a Saturday and Sunday keeps that cost close to zero: the crew clears the old office, sets the new one out to the floor plan, and your people arrive on Monday to desks, chairs and screens already in place.

Weekends also help with the physical side. Traffic is lighter, loading bays are quieter, and buildings have fewer tenants coming and going. Weekend slots do carry a premium over Monday to Friday daytime rates, so weigh that against what a weekday closure would cost you. For most offices the sums are not close.

## Start the timeline eight weeks out

Six to eight weeks is a comfortable lead time for a small to medium office. Larger or multi-floor moves need longer. Work back from the move weekend:

- Eight weeks out: confirm the date with both landlords, book the removal company and arrange a survey of both addresses.
- Six weeks out: agree the new floor plan and desk allocation, and tell your IT provider the date.
- Four weeks out: confirm building access, lifts and loading bays at both ends, and request any parking suspensions.
- Two weeks out: crates delivered, labelling system issued, staff briefed.
- Move week: IT disconnect plan signed off, final walkthrough with the move coordinator.

## Sort building access at both addresses

London commercial buildings are managed, and management rarely works weekends by default. Speak to the facilities team at both addresses and get the following in writing:

- Out of hours access: who unlocks the building, who is on site and what the security arrangements are.
- Goods lift: whether it needs booking, whether it is locked off at weekends and whether a porter must operate it.
- Loading bay: opening hours, maximum vehicle height and length, and whether another tenant has already reserved it.
- Protection: some buildings require floor protection, lift padding or a method statement and risk assessment (RAMS) before they allow a move. Ask early, because this paperwork takes time.

Where there is no usable loading bay, the vehicle will have to park on the street. In most central boroughs that means a council parking suspension on the bays outside the building, applied for several working days in advance, so this is not a job for the Friday before. A good removal company will handle the application as part of planning, but they need your go-ahead in good time.

If either address sits inside the Congestion Charge zone, check the hours that apply on the weekend you are moving. The same goes for the Ultra Low Emission Zone, although a professional fleet should already be compliant.

## Plan the IT move first, not last

Nothing stalls a Monday morning like a server still in a crate. IT should be the first thing you plan and the first thing that moves.

Your IT lead or managed service provider should own the disconnection on Friday evening and the reconnection on Sunday. Agree the sequence with them: what powers down first, how it is labelled, and what must be running before staff arrive. Then share it with the removal coordinator so the crew packs and loads in the same order.

A few practical points:

- Back up everything before anything is unplugged. Then check the backup actually restores.
- Photograph the back of every rack and desk setup before cables come out.
- Label cables by desk number, not by person. People move desks; numbers do not.
- Confirm the new building's internet connection is live and tested before the weekend. New circuits in London can take weeks, and no amount of good packing fixes a line that has not been installed.

## Crates, labelling and who packs what

For an office move, plastic crates beat cardboard boxes almost every time. They stack safely, protect files and monitors better, load faster and go back at the end rather than into a skip. Lidded crates can be hired for the move and collected once you have unpacked; see the [crate hire](/crate-hire/) page.

Decide early whether staff pack their own desks or the removal crew does it. Self-packing is cheaper on paper but depends on everyone doing it on time and properly. For larger offices, or where a late start on Monday is not an option, a [packing service](/packing-service/) that handles files, equipment and furniture is often the safer choice.

Whichever route you take, use one labelling system across the whole office:

- Number every desk on the new floor plan and give every crate, chair and monitor its destination desk number.
- Use colour-coded labels per department or floor so crews can sort by eye.
- Keep a master list of what is in each numbered crate for anything that matters, such as contracts or HR files.
- Mark anything that is not moving with a clear "do not move" label.

## Brief your staff properly

People make or break a weekend move. A short written brief, sent two weeks before and repeated in move week, should cover:

- What each person must do before leaving on Friday: clear their desk, pack and label their crate, and unplug their own peripherals if that is the plan.
- What they must take home: personal items, valuables and plants.
- Where they are sitting on Monday, with a floor plan.
- Who to call on Monday if something is missing or not working.

Give one or two people the job of being on site over the weekend, or at least reachable by phone. The crew will have questions only the business can answer, and a two-minute decision on Saturday saves a crate of unknown files sitting in the wrong corner for a week.

## Clearance, storage and the leftovers

Every office move surfaces things nobody wants to take: old chairs, broken printers, archive boxes from a decade ago. Decide what happens to each before the weekend, not on it.

Decommissioned furniture and IT can be collected by a licensed waste carrier, with electrical items going through WEEE-compliant recycling. Anything you want to keep but have no room for yet can go into [business storage](/storage/). ## The Monday morning check

Before anyone else arrives on Monday, walk the new office with your floor plan. Confirm desks are in the right place, screens are powered and the network is up. Check the phones and the printer, because people notice those first. Keep a list of snags and give it to the move coordinator the same day.
